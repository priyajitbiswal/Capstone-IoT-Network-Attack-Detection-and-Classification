\begin{abstract}
Machine learning (ML) models evaluated on modern Internet of Things (IoT) network intrusion detection benchmarks, particularly CICIoT2023, routinely report near-perfect classification accuracies exceeding 98.5\% to 99.9\%. In this paper, we conduct a forensic data integrity audit and empirical benchmark demonstrating that these headline metrics are heavily distorted by unpartitioned identical-feature data leakage, unacknowledged conflicting label collisions, severe class imbalance, and an absence of cross-session temporal evaluation. Across a stratified sample of 529,562 flow records, we identify 11.29\% feature redundancy (59,780 flows) and 8,740 conflicting-label collision hashes spanning 28,608 rows (5.40\% of the sample), where mathematically identical 39-feature coordinate vectors map to contradictory attack and benign classes. We demonstrate that conventional random train/test splits allow 81.39\% of test collision instances to leak directly from the training set, transforming evaluation into rote nearest-neighbor memorization. To establish an authentic baseline, we introduce an atomic SHA-256 group-hash partitioning protocol guaranteeing strictly zero inter-partition feature leakage alongside strict preprocessor isolation. We evaluate five representative model families across 21 configurations spanning Binary (2-class), Category (8-class), and Fine-Grained (34-class) tasks. Under zero leakage, top gradient-boosted decision trees achieve realistic diagnostic baselines: 89.61\% binary accuracy (Random Forest, Macro-F1 0.7690), 78.93\% category accuracy (LightGBM, Macro-F1 0.6894), and 72.65\% fine-grained accuracy (LightGBM, Macro-F1 0.6145). We uncover an operational ``Accuracy Paradox'': unweighted training artificially inflates overall test accuracy (+1.80\%) by favoring volumetric majority floods, but triggers a catastrophic 79\% to 82\% relative recall collapse on stealthy web attacks (SQL Injection: 35.83\% $\to$ 7.48\%; XSS: 20.22\% $\to$ 3.83\%) and surges false negatives to Benign by +125.31\% (1,517 $\to$ 3,418). Finally, temporal evaluation on an independent clean cross-capture holdout ($N=57,280$) recorded during subsequent physical testbed sessions reveals substantial performance degradation (binary Macro-F1 drops from 0.7637 to 0.7034), demonstrating that models fit session-specific burst dynamics. Our code, partitioning pipeline, and evaluation artifacts are made fully reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) infrastructure has dramatically expanded the enterprise attack surface. Billions of edge devices---including smart home sensors, surveillance endpoints, industrial actuators, and healthcare gateways---are directly interfaced with public or unsegmented local networks. Due to severe hardware footprint constraints, legacy unauthenticated protocols, and rare firmware patching schedules, IoT networks are prime targets for malicious actors. Threats range from multi-gigabit Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) botnets (such as Mirai and its variants) to stealthy, low-footprint application exploits including SQL Injection (SQLi), Cross-Site Scripting (XSS), Command Injection, and unauthorized remote backdoor execution~\cite{neto2023ciciot, ahmad2021network}.

To defend these heterogeneous perimeters, network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) increasingly rely on machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) models trained on tabular flow telemetry~\cite{mishra2019detailed}. The research community has enthusiastically adopted modern large-scale benchmarks, foremost among them the \textbf{CICIoT2023} dataset published by the Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity~\cite{neto2023ciciot}. Comprising over 46 million raw network flows generated on a physical testbed of 105 IoT devices across 33 attack profiles, CICIoT2023 is widely hailed as the gold standard for IoT security benchmarking.

\subsection{The ``Accuracy Paradox'' in IoT Benchmarks}
In recent literature, published empirical evaluations on CICIoT2023 routinely claim near-perfect diagnostic capability. As documented in our literature survey (Section~\ref{sec:related_work}), recent peer-reviewed studies report classification accuracies consistently spanning **98.5\% to 99.9\%** across diverse classifiers including Random Forests, XGBoost, LightGBM, 1D-CNNs, and hybrid Transformer networks~\cite{neto2023ciciot, khan2026resource, almahaqeri2026optimized, ullah2026comparative, abdelrahman2026feature}.

However, there is a stark dissonance between these reported laboratory metrics and practical operational realities. When deployed in live networks, ML-based NIDS frequently experience high false-positive rates on normal traffic, total blindness to low-rate application exploits, and severe diagnostic decay over time. In this paper, we identify three structural phenomena that explain this disparity:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High Feature Redundancy and Random-Split Leakage}: Raw flow datasets contain massive numbers of repeated and duplicate feature vectors. When researchers employ standard naive random train/test splits, identical feature vectors are randomly assigned to both partitions. Classifiers evaluate on samples they have essentially memorized during training, artificially inflating published accuracy metrics.
    \item \textbf{The Masking Effect of Extreme Class Imbalance}: Volumetric flooding attacks (e.g., UDP/SYN floods) account for over 85\% of raw packets, whereas critical application attacks (e.g., SQL Injection, XSS) represent less than 0.05\%. A naive classifier that maximizes aggregate accuracy can completely ignore stealthy attacks while still reporting $>$98\% overall accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Temporal Validation}: Typical benchmark evaluations test models on random holdout slices drawn from the exact same network capture session. This fails to assess whether classifiers learn robust attack signatures or merely fit the transient, session-specific burst characteristics of the experimental testbed.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Questions and Contributions}
To resolve these issues, this paper formulates the following \textbf{Central Research Question}:
\begin{quote}
\textit{When identical-feature data leakage and class imbalance artifacts are rigorously controlled through atomic hash-grouped partitioning and cost-sensitive optimization, what is the realistic baseline diagnostic capability, minority attack recall, and temporal stability of standard machine learning architectures on the CICIoT2023 benchmark?}
\end{quote}

We investigate this question through five structured Sub-Research Questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Integrity)}: To what extent do identical feature vectors, exact duplicates, and conflicting label collisions exist in CICIoT2023, and what fraction of test instances leak under naive random splitting?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Zero-Leakage Baseline)}: What are the authentic diagnostic boundaries of linear, tree, and neural baselines across Binary (2-class), Category (8-class), and Fine-Grained (34-class) tasks when feature leakage is strictly eliminated?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Ambiguity Mechanics)}: How does the presence of conflicting label collisions affect tree and linear models, and does collision purging improve clean generalization?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Operational Trade-Offs)}: How does standard unweighted empirical loss minimization trade minority attack detection for overall headline accuracy?
    \item \textbf{RQ5 (Temporal Fragility)}: How robust are trained models when evaluated on an independent capture session recorded across subsequent hours on the physical IoT testbed?
\end{itemize}

This manuscript delivers \textbf{four consolidated research contributions}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Forensic Data Integrity and Leakage Audit}: We perform a granular census of 529,562 stratified CICIoT2023 flow records. We discover that 11.29\% of flows share repeated feature vectors, and 8,740 unique 39-feature coordinates represent \textit{conflicting label collisions} spanning 28,608 rows (where identical vectors carry conflicting attack and benign labels). We prove empirically that naive 80/20 random stratified splitting leaks 81.39\% of test collision instances directly from the training set.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible Zero-Leakage Group-Hash Partitioning Standard}: We formulate an open-source atomic SHA-256 group-hash partitioning protocol guaranteeing strictly zero identical-feature overlap ($\mathcal{X}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{X}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$) alongside strict preprocessor isolation. Across 21 models and three granularities, our partitions exhibit rock-solid stability ($|\Delta_{\text{gen}}| \le 0.0079$), establishing verified realistic baselines (89.61\% binary, 78.93\% category, 72.65\% fine-grained).
    \item \textbf{Dissection of Ambiguity Filtering Mechanics}: We demonstrate that Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) are inherently robust to conflicting label noise. Decomposed validation on 72,390 common-clean instances shows that purging collision vectors yields only a marginal +0.0022 Macro-F1 improvement (+0.22\%), while classifiers achieve ~41.73\% accuracy on isolated ambiguous vectors due to the non-injective nature of the stripped feature space.
    \item \textbf{Quantitative Trade-Offs: Minority Recall Collapse and Temporal Non-Stationarity}: We expose the operational ``Accuracy Paradox'': unweighted training raises headline accuracy by +1.80\% but triggers a catastrophic 79\% to 82\% relative recall collapse on stealthy web attacks and surges missed intrusions to Benign by +125.31\%. Furthermore, temporal evaluation across distinct physical capture sessions reveals marked performance degradation (binary Macro-F1 drops from 0.7637 to 0.7034), disproving assumptions of session invariance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Research Gap Analysis}
\label{sec:related_work}

Network Intrusion Detection Systems for IoT environments have been extensively studied across diverse benchmarks, including NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, BoT-IoT, ToN\_IoT, and CICIoT2023~\cite{ahmad2021network, mishra2019detailed, booij2022toniot}.

\subsection{Thematic Audit of Recent Literature}
To contextualize our findings, we conducted a systematic methodological audit of 14 peer-reviewed research papers published between 2021 and 2026 evaluating IoT NIDS models (detailed in Table~\ref{tab:lit_matrix}). These works fall into four primary methodological themes:

\subsubsection{Theme A: Tree Ensembles and Feature Selection}
Gradient-boosted decision trees (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) and Random Forests remain dominant for tabular flow analysis due to their non-linear decision boundaries and high inference speed. Khan et al.~\cite{khan2026resource} combined Boruta feature selection with Greedy Forward Selection (GFS) on CICIoT2023 and ToN\_IoT, pruning feature sets to 10--15 attributes while reporting 99.72\%--99.94\% accuracy. Almahaqeri et al.~\cite{almahaqeri2026optimized} evaluated optimized GBDTs on CICIoT2023, reporting 99.61\% binary accuracy and 0.355\,$\mu$s latency. Abdelrahman et al.~\cite{abdelrahman2026feature} introduced FEC-IDS, achieving 99.76\% binary and 98.88\% 34-class accuracy using tree-based feature rankings. While these works successfully reduce feature dimensionalities, none audited identical-feature vector collisions across splits or evaluated temporal session stability.

\subsubsection{Theme B: Deep Sequence and Recurrent Architectures}
Several studies explore deep learning to model temporal or inter-feature correlations. Gulzar and Mustafa~\cite{gulzar2025enhancing} proposed DeepCLG (combining 1D-CNN, LSTM, and GRU layers), achieving 99.82\% accuracy on BoT-IoT but experiencing performance drops down to 57.77\% on UNSW-NB15. Qaddos et al.~\cite{qaddos2024novel} deployed a 1D-CNN-BiLSTM architecture with attention mechanisms, reporting $>$99\% accuracy on BoT-IoT. While deep architectures offer expressive representation learning, their high compute overhead ($>$15\,ms latency) severely challenges edge gateway constraints, and their evaluations were restricted to in-distribution random splits.

\subsubsection{Theme C: Federated Learning and Edge Privacy}
Federated learning (FL) frameworks train collaborative NIDS without aggregating raw network telemetry. Moussaoui et al.~\cite{moussaoui2026resilient} evaluated a CNN-LSTM architecture under Dirichlet non-IID client distributions on CICIoT2023, observing that F1 dropped from 97.36\% in centralized training to 71.53\% under extreme client skew ($\alpha=0.01$). Al-Hejri et al.~\cite{alhejri2026improving} combined CNNs with Vision Transformers in an FL pipeline, reporting a drop from 94.89\% to 69.35\% under non-IID conditions. Fenanir and Semchedine~\cite{fenanir2023smart} and Ullah et al.~\cite{ullah2026comparative} explored edge federated aggregation across IoT gateways. These studies highlight the sensitivity of classifiers to distribution shifts, though they did not explore cross-capture temporal drift on identical testbeds.

\subsubsection{Theme D: Benchmark Generation and Baselining}
The foundational CICIoT2023 study by Neto et al.~\cite{neto2023ciciot} benchmarked eight ML/DL algorithms across 105 devices, reporting 99.16\% accuracy with Random Forest. However, their DNN baseline exhibited severe minority attack starvation, detecting only 14.7\% of Dictionary Brute Force and 3.8\% of Web attacks. Booij et al.~\cite{booij2022toniot} and Moustafa~\cite{moustafa2021distributed} evaluated ToN\_IoT, demonstrating that models trained with static IP addresses fail to generalize across network environments. Essop et al.~\cite{essop2021dataset} and Umar et al.~\cite{umar2025energy} addressed dataset generation fidelity and knowledge distillation for edge deployment.

\subsection{The Narrowly Supported Research Gap}
Our systematic audit establishes a critical methodological gap across the 14 surveyed studies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Zero Collision Audits}: \textit{None of the 14 surveyed studies} performed an identical-feature vector audit or identified conflicting label collisions across splits. Several studies pruned exact duplicate full rows ($X, y$), but overlooked identical feature coordinates ($X$) carrying conflicting labels ($y$).
    \item \textbf{Absence of Temporal Cross-Capture Validation}: \textit{None of the 14 surveyed studies} evaluated trained models on distinct temporal capture sessions recorded on the same physical testbed. All evaluations relied on random in-distribution holdouts or cross-validation.
    \item \textbf{Conflation of Metric Types}: Several works reported near-perfect aggregate accuracy without isolating minority attack recall, masking severe false-negative rates on stealthy threats.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Dataset Integrity, Collisions, and Leakage Analysis}
\label{sec:dataset_integrity}

\subsection{The CICIoT2023 Benchmark and Feature Space}
The CICIoT2023 dataset was generated by deploying 105 IoT devices executing 33 distinct attack profiles and benign network behaviors. Network traffic was captured via port mirroring and processed into bidirectional flow statistics. While raw packet captures contain link-layer and network-layer identifiers (MAC addresses, IP addresses, port numbers, and timestamps), the benchmark feature extractor deliberately stripped these identifiers to prevent machine learning models from learning static shortcut rules (e.g., memorizing attacker IP addresses)~\cite{neto2023ciciot}.

The resulting public tabular schema consists of \textbf{39 numerical features} plus a ground-truth label. The features capture:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Flow Rates}: Total flow duration, packet rate, and byte rate.
    \item \textit{Packet Length Statistics}: Minimum, maximum, mean, and standard deviation of packet sizes.
    \item \textit{Protocol Headers}: TCP, UDP, ICMP, HTTP, and TLS flag indicators and header length metrics.
    \item \textit{TCP State Dynamics}: FIN, SYN, RST, PSH, ACK, ECE, and CWR flag counts and window sizes.
    \item \textit{Inter-Arrival Times (IAT)}: Mean, variance, and maximum time between consecutive packets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Leakage-Controlled Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Atomic SHA-256 Hash-Grouped Partitioning}
To permanently eliminate identical-feature data leakage, we introduce an **Atomic Hash-Grouped Partitioning Protocol**. 
Let $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{39}$ denote the feature vector of flow $i$. We compute a 64-character SHA-256 hash digest over the canonical string representation of $\mathbf{x}_i$:
\begin{equation}
h_i = \text{SHA-256}\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{39} \text{repr}(x_{i,j})\right)
\end{equation}
All flows sharing the identical hash $h_i$ form an atomic group $\mathcal{G}_k$. Each atomic group is assigned in its entirety to either the training set ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$), validation set ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$), or test set ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$) using a stratified hash assignment algorithm that preserves class proportions while strictly guaranteeing:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{X}_{\text{val}} = \emptyset, \quad \mathcal{X}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{X}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset, \quad \mathcal{X}_{\text{val}} \cap \mathcal{X}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset
\end{equation}

\subsection{Partition Formulations: Strategy 1 vs. Strategy 2}
We formulate two controlled partitioning regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strategy 1 (Primary / Collision-Contaminated)}: Retains all 501,098 unique stratum flows, partitioning them into Train ($N = 350,821$; 70\%), Validation ($N = 75,172$; 15\%), and Internal Test ($N = 75,105$; 15\%). This reflects real-world deployment where conflicting label noise exists naturally in network traffic.
    \item \textbf{Strategy 2 (Ablation / Collision-Purged)}: Completely purges all 8,740 conflicting collision hashes, leaving 482,682 flows partitioned into Train ($N = 337,838$), Validation ($N = 72,390$), and Internal Test ($N = 72,454$).
\end{itemize}

Crucially, the validation set of Strategy 2 ($N = 72,390$) represents the exact **Common-Clean Validation Population** embedded within Strategy 1. The remaining 2,782 validation flows in Strategy 1 constitute the **Ambiguous Collision Validation Population**, allowing precise decomposed error routing.

\subsection{Temporal Cross-Capture Clean Holdout}
To evaluate temporal generalizability, we extracted an independent holdout set of \textbf{$N = 57,280$ flows} from subsequent capture archives (`Merged26.csv` through `Merged40.csv`). These files were recorded during later operational hours on the same physical IoT testbed. We verified that the cross-capture holdout shares **zero identical-feature overlap ($0.00\%$)** with the training partition.

\subsection{Preprocessor Isolation and Model Configurations}
To prevent distributional leakage, all preprocessing transformations---including finite rate clipping limits and `StandardScaler` moments ($\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\sigma}^2$)---were fitted strictly on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$ and serialized. Model evaluation verified exact parity ($\Delta = 0.00000$) on test splits.

We benchmarked five representative model families across three diagnostic granularities (21 configurations total):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{SGD Logistic Regression}: Linear classifier trained via mini-batch stochastic gradient descent with log-loss and $L_2$ regularization.
    \item \textbf{PyTorch Tabular MLP}: Multi-layer perceptron (Input[39] $\to$ Dense[128] $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ Dropout[0.2] $\to$ Dense[64] $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ Output[$K$]) trained with AdamW under a standard 5-epoch CPU budget.
    \item \textbf{Random Forest (RF)}: Ensemble of 50 decorrelated decision trees with max depth 15 and balanced bootstrapping~\cite{breiman2001random}.
    \item \textbf{LightGBM}: Leaf-wise gradient boosted trees with GOSS sampling and max depth 12~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}.
    \item \textbf{XGBoost}: Depth-wise gradient boosted trees with exact split finding and histogram binning~\cite{chen2016xgboost}.
\end{enumerate}

To counter extreme class imbalance, models were optimized using cost-sensitive inverse-frequency class weighting:
\begin{equation}
W_c = \frac{N_{\text{train}}}{K \cdot N_c}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of classes and $N_c$ is the support of class $c$.

\section{Discussion and Practical Implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Re-Evaluating Published Performance on CICIoT2023}
Our empirical findings provide an evidence-based explanation for the discrepancy between published $>$99\% accuracies and practical NIDS operational failures. Prior evaluations suffered from the compounding effects of:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Rote Memorization}: Under unpartitioned random splits, classifiers exploit the 81.39\% collision leakage to memorize coordinate-to-label mappings.
    \item \textit{Metric Distortion}: High-volume flooding traffic masks near-zero detection rates on stealthy exploits when evaluations rely on overall accuracy.
    \item \textit{Temporal Session Overfitting}: Testing on slices of the training session overlooks concept drift and burst non-stationarity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Recommendations for IoT NIDS Deployment}
Based on our findings, we formulate four actionable recommendations for designing and evaluating IoT intrusion detection systems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mandate Hash-Grouped Partitioning}: Evaluators must verify that $\mathcal{X}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{X}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$. Standard random splits on tabular flow data must be treated as methodologically invalid due to severe leakage risks.
    \item \textbf{Prioritize Cost-Sensitive Loss Objectives}: Systems designers must reject pure aggregate accuracy in favor of balanced macro metrics and minority recall bounds to ensure protection against low-rate exploits.
    \item \textbf{Institute Multi-Tier Detection Cascades}: Network architects should deploy lightweight linear scrubbers (e.g., SGD) at edge ingress to filter obvious volumetric floods at wire speed ($>$2.9M flows/sec), routing ambiguous flows to GBDT classifiers for fine-grained analysis.
    \item \textbf{Integrate Selective Deep Packet Inspection}: Tabular flow statistics are fundamentally blind to application-layer attack payloads. Flow classifiers should be coupled with targeted deep packet inspection (DPI) for HTTP and TLS streams.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:threats}

We explicitly delineate the scientific boundaries of this investigation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset Scope}: All empirical benchmarks were conducted on the CICIoT2023 dataset. While CICIoT2023 represents a diverse 105-device physical testbed, cross-dataset transferability to ToN\_IoT or BoT-IoT was not benchmarked.
    \item \textbf{Payload Inaccessibility}: The dataset strips packet payloads and raw byte streams. Misclassifications on SQL Injection and XSS reflect the inherent limitation of distinguishing web attacks using statistical flow counters alone.
    \item \textbf{Neural Training Budget}: The PyTorch MLP baseline was trained under a 5-epoch CPU budget. Extended GPU training with advanced architectures (e.g., TabNet) may yield higher neural accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Host Hardware Profiling}: Latency and throughput were measured on an Intel Core i5 host CPU. Performance on constrained microcontrollers (ARM Cortex-M) remains subject to physical edge hardware constraints.
    \item \textbf{Literature Review Scope}: Methodological comparisons are bounded strictly to the 14 surveyed peer-reviewed studies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper conducted a rigorous data integrity audit and empirical benchmark of machine learning models on the CICIoT2023 benchmark. We demonstrated that headline accuracies exceeding 99\% reported in prior literature are vulnerable to unpartitioned identical-feature data leakage (81.39\% collision leakage) and class imbalance distortion. By introducing an atomic SHA-256 group-hash partitioning standard, we established authentic zero-leakage diagnostic baselines (89.61\% binary, 78.93\% category, and 72.65\% fine-grained accuracy). We uncovered an operational ``Accuracy Paradox'', showing that unweighted training trades a 79\% to 82\% collapse in minority attack recall for a minor +1.80\% gain in headline accuracy. Furthermore, temporal cross-capture evaluation revealed substantial session fragility. Future work will investigate physical microcontroller hardware deployment (ARM Cortex-M / Raspberry Pi), multi-modal tabular-payload fusion architectures, and cross-dataset transferability across heterogeneous IoT environments.
